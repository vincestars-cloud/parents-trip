Read first: the Abu Dhabi question

Since the US and Iran began fighting in February, the US government has told Americans to “reconsider travel” to the United Arab Emirates, where Abu Dhabi is. Flights are running and the city is open, but delays or worse are more likely than normal, and insurance does not cover war. Thailand has no warning at all.

1. Keep the planEtihad through Abu Dhabi with the free night. Cheapest. Most exposed to the warning.
2. Etihad, no stopSame tickets, 2 hours in the airport each way. You still pass through twice.
3. Avoid the Middle EastUnited through San Francisco and Hong Kong. About $280 more, 27 hours each way, no free hotel.

Sources: the US Embassy in the UAE, 29 August 2026.

Before you fly
  • Passports valid past 20 March 2027. Check the expiry line today.
  • Insurance the same week as the flights: IMG iTravelInsured Choice or Trawick Safe Travels Voyager, about $105 to $140 each. Medicare pays nothing abroad.
  • Two shots: Hepatitis A and typhoid. Any pharmacy. Hepatitis A needs two weeks to work.
  • Thailand arrival form, free, online, 17 to 20 September. Vince will do it.
  • Medicines in the original bottles, plus a week extra, with a printed list.
  • Apps: Grab (the taxi app), Airalo (phone service), Mobile Passport Control, WhatsApp.
Phones

An eSIM is a phone plan you download instead of a card you push in. Buy it in the Airalo app at home on Wi-Fi. It switches on when you land.

  • The cheap way: one eSIM on Dad’s phone ($15.50 for both countries) with “Personal Hotspot” on for Mom’s phone.
  • Does my phone do it? iPhone XS or newer, Samsung S20 or newer. Dial *#06# on an iPhone: if you see “EID”, yes.
  • If not: a SIM card at the Bangkok airport, open all night, about $15. Bring the passport.
  • WhatsApp: works in Thailand. In Abu Dhabi, texts and photos work, calls do not.
Money

100 baht is about $3. 1,000 baht is about $30. A street lunch is 60 to 100 baht. A taxi across Bangkok is 150 to 200.

  • Cash machines charge $7 every time. Take out the maximum once. Always choose “charge in baht”, never dollars.
  • Open a Charles Schwab checking account now. It refunds every one of those fees. The card takes two weeks to arrive.
  • Bring $300 to $500 in new $100 bills. Change them at SuperRich in the airport basement, not upstairs.
  • Cards work at hotels, hospitals, malls and the taxi app. Cash for markets, street food, massages.
  • Tips: not expected in Thailand. Round up taxis. 50 to 100 baht after a massage.
Buying oud, gold and dates (for Dad)

Two markets, ten minutes apart. Do them in this order on the Saturday.

  • Mina Zayed first, 8 AM. Open-air, so go while it is cool. Dates, spices, fish, carpets, the Iranian souk. 19 September is peak fresh date harvest — the best week of the year to buy them. Dates ask $5 to $27 a kilo; expect to pay about half after bargaining. They vacuum-seal for the flight.
  • Madinat Zayed at 9 AM. Indoor, air-conditioned, about 31 perfume shops and 61 jewellers, and every jeweller is on one flat ground floor. Good toilets, taxis at every exit. Rated 4.3 from 31,000 reviews and it is not a tourist place.
  • Skip Souk Qaryat Al Beri and the World Trade Center souk. Both open at 10 and Qaryat Al Beri's shops are shut during the day: it is an evening place, and you fly at 9 PM.

How to tell real oud from perfumed oil. Real oud oil costs about $39 to $65 a millilitre. A 12 ml bottle at $14 is a thirty-fifth of that price, so it is a carrier oil with a synthetic scent, whatever the label says. Bottles labelled “Dehn Al Oud” run from $0.40 to $65 a millilitre — a 164-fold spread for the same words.

  • The test: one drop on the back of the hand, then wait ten minutes. Real oud keeps changing as it dries. A synthetic smells identical at minute one and minute thirty.
  • A genuine seller will heat a chip for you, show you the raw wood, name where it came from, and be relaxed if you walk out.
  • Walk away if they put it on you before you agree, name a price only after you have committed, say it is the last one today, or mix it in the back where you cannot watch. And if the price drops from $500 to $80 without a pause, it was never real.
  • Bargain about 25% on ordinary perfume. Do not bargain hard on real oud: that is the one line where the price is the price.

Two rules about bringing it home.

  • Charcoal discs are banned from the plane — cabin and checked, both. They are a flammable solid. Buy the incense and the burner; buy charcoal at home, or buy an electric burner instead.
  • There is a legal limit on oud: 24 ml of oil per person, or 1 kg of wood chips. Agarwood is a protected species. Two small bottles each is fine; a big bottle is not.
  • Oil in hand luggage must be in bottles of 100 ml or less, all in one clear bag. The 3, 6 and 12 ml bottles they sell are no problem.

Buying gold. The gold price per gram is fixed for the whole country and posted daily, so nobody can beat it. The only negotiable part is the making charge. Ask two questions: “What is today's rate per gram for this karat?” then “What is the making charge per gram?” Ask 20 to 30% off the making charge; that is normal and usually accepted. Beware “no making charge” offers — it is hidden in a higher gram price.

Get the tax back. There is 5% VAT. On anything over about $68, ask for the tax-free tag in the shop, then validate it at the Planet desk in Abu Dhabi departures (in front of entrances 1 and 8) before you check in. You get most of the VAT back in cash.

Getting around

Grab is Uber. Type where you are going, see the price, a car comes. Set it up at home; the code arrives by WhatsApp.

  • Bangkok: Grab across town, $3 to $7. The Skytrain for straight runs, $1, tap a card at the gate. The river ferry, 15 cents.
  • Chiang Mai: the Old City is flat; most things are a walk. Grab for the evenings, $2 to $3.
  • Abu Dhabi: metered taxis from the rank at every door, $10 to $20 a hop.
  • Never a tuk-tuk ride, and never a driver’s “recommended” shop.
The airports
  • Atlanta: international terminal, 3 hours early. TSA Cares, (855) 787-2227, meets you at security if you call 72 hours ahead.
  • Abu Dhabi: one new building. Free passport stamp; ask for the assistance lane. Your suitcases come out on the way there and are checked again on Saturday.
  • Bangkok: show the arrival-form QR code. Then the metered taxi queue on Level 1, $15 to $20. Ignore anyone offering a “limousine”.
  • Chiang Mai: turn left in arrivals to the taxi counter, $5.
  • Coming home: open the Mobile Passport Control app on the plane and use its lane at Atlanta.
The hospital and the dentist

Bumrungrad is like Emory: big, modern, every doctor speaks English. The difference is it is one minute from the hotel, the check-up costs a third of the US price, and the doctor goes through the results the same afternoon.

  • Check-up: nothing to eat after 7 PM Sunday. 7 AM Monday. Done by early afternoon. Printed copy Wednesday.
  • Dentist: across the road. Crown prepared Monday, fitted Wednesday. About $680 to $830.
  • An implant cannot be finished on this trip. The post must heal for months. Pull the tooth or place the post now, finish next visit.
  • Bring: recent US lab results, the dentist’s x-rays and quote, the medicine list.
  • Nothing surgical on Wednesday. Dentists want two to three days before flying.
Heat, rain and walking

Bangkok in September feels like Atlanta in late July, with a one-hour shower most afternoons. Abu Dhabi is hotter than Georgia ever gets.

  • Outdoors before 10:30 AM or after 4 PM. Midday is pool, museum or nap.
  • A big glass of water before leaving. A bottle each in the bag.
  • Nothing in the plan asks for more than 15 minutes of walking at a stretch.
  • Golf cart at the mosque, cable car on the mountain, wheelchairs at every airport. All free. Ask.
What to wear
  • The mosque: ankle-length, long sleeves, both of you. A headscarf for Stella. Bring your own.
  • Temples: shoulders and knees covered. Shoes off at every building, so slip-ons.
  • Abu Dhabi in general: shoulders and knees covered in malls. No photos of people, government buildings or the airport.
  • Everyday Thailand: light, loose, a hat. Nobody dresses up.
Tricks to ignore
  • “The palace is closed today.” It is open every day. Walk past.
  • The “government gem sale” and the tailor’s “special price”. No such things.
  • Free bird seed, surveys, “I just want to practise my English”.
  • Wrong change on a 1,000-baht note. Pay with 100s and count at the counter.
  • Tourist police, English-speaking, for anything: 1155.

Packing
  • Light, loose, long clothes. Two long-sleeved shirts each. A scarf. Hats. Slip-on shoes. Sandals that can get wet. A light rain jacket. A sweater for the plane.
  • Medicines in original bottles plus a week. Reading glasses. Sunscreen. Electrolyte sachets.
  • Passports, printed bookings, insurance card, the medicine list, the dental x-rays.
  • One British-style three-pin adapter for Abu Dhabi. Thailand takes US plugs. A power bank. Earphones.
  • Neck pillow, eye mask, compression socks for the long flights, a refillable bottle, snacks you know.
